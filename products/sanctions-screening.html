MCPize version

Bring potential sanctions matches and their source evidence into one review workflow. On MCPize, screen up to 10 subjects per call against five official lists: OFAC SDN, OFAC Consolidated, EU, UK and UN. Re-screen up to 50 subjects against prior results supplied by the caller, export a cached list, or inspect list status.

Capabilities, limits and platform options

The MCPize surface does not include OpenSanctions or a comprehensive PEP database. List data is cached; a monitor call does not force a new download and stores no caller history. Ownership signals do not calculate beneficial-ownership percentages. Results require human review. The current UK/EU source parsers are deployed, but hosted source loading and screening remain under verification. Complete hosted tool checks are still gated by consumer access. Do not rely on a CLEAR result for compliance decisions while this check is pending.

MCPize supports 10-subject screening calls and 50-subject re-checks with separate per-tool pricing. Apify uses the same five-source scope with different bulk-run limits. See current MCPize plans and setup.

Apify status: the coverage guard, current UK source migration and five-source refresh were verified on 31 October 2026. Empty, stale, missing or explicitly unsupported source coverage cannot produce a CLEAR result. Legacy OpenSanctions requests are rejected instead of silently screening fewer sources.

Apify version

The Apify workflow.

Screens any name or company against the current OFAC, EU, UK and UN sanctions and watchlists - official sources only.

Every match comes back with a deterministic fuzzy-match score and full detail, so nothing is a black-box yes/no.

Works the same for a single check from a form or a bulk list run overnight.

The same person or company is frequently listed independently by more than one authority. Rather than returning four rows to reconcile by hand, matches are consolidated into a single identity carrying every list it appears on, with the programme and source URL for each.

A bare similarity score is hard to act on, so every result carries a documented verdict and a recommended action. False-positive analysis surfaces mismatching dates of birth, countries, nationalities and identifiers explicitly, so an analyst can see why a high-scoring match is probably the wrong person.

Screening results are written before any audit certificate is generated, so a certificate failure can never cost you the screening work you already paid for. That ordering exists because of a real incident, and it is deliberate.

Both shopfronts are scoped to five direct official sources: OFAC SDN, OFAC Consolidated, EU, the current FCDO UK Sanctions List and UN. Neither claims comprehensive PEP coverage. The Apify Actor's current five-source refresh and supported screening path are verified; MCPize hosted verification remains pending.

Who it's for

  • For compliance and risk teams. Screen a new counterparty at onboarding and get a documented CLEAR, REVIEW or ESCALATE verdict with false-positive analysis, instead of four separate list lookups reconciled by hand.
  • For finance and accounts payable. Check a payee before releasing a wire, including the crypto wallet address when that is the payment rail, so a blocked party is caught before funds move.
  • For operations and procurement. Screen a supplier list before a contract round, then re-screen on a schedule so a supplier who becomes listed after onboarding surfaces on the next check rather than at audit.
  • For developers and AI agents. Call it inside a KYC intake or payment-release flow, branch on the verdict, and escalate only REVIEW and ESCALATE to a person.
